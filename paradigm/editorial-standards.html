Editorial Standards

Paradigm publishes several distinct kinds of material side by side — official guidance, Arena's own analysis, discussion papers, and (in future) community discussion. Keeping these visually and structurally distinct is the single most important editorial commitment on this site: a reader should never have to guess which kind of material they're looking at, or mistake Arena's interpretation for the guidance itself.

Purpose of Paradigm

Paradigm exists to encourage evidence-led professional discussion around educational technology, safeguarding, governance, AI, SEND, Alternative Provision and educational policy — in a space that is deliberately separate from Arena's product pages. It is not a marketing site, and no paper is written or edited with the aim of selling anything. Where a paper's argument genuinely touches on how Arena's own products approach a problem, that connection is disclosed openly in a clearly marked Arena Commentary section, never folded into the main analysis.

Editorial process

Every paper is written or commissioned by Arena, attributed to a named author, dated on first publication, and checked against the official guidance and external sources it references before it is published. Papers currently go through single-author review by Arena's founder — a genuine limitation for a young research library, and one we would rather state plainly than obscure. Expanding review beyond a single author, as Paradigm grows, is an explicit goal rather than an afterthought.

Document review process

A paper is reviewed for factual accuracy and continued relevance whenever the official guidance it discusses is updated, and at minimum annually from its last review date. Review does not mean a paper is rewritten each time — most reviews simply confirm the paper's argument still holds and its citations are current. Where review identifies that a paper's position needs updating, the version-control methodology below governs how that change is made and recorded.

Version control methodology

Each paper carries a version number, starting at 1.0 on first publication. A substantive revision — new guidance, new evidence, or a material correction — increases the version number and is accompanied by a short note describing what changed and why. Minor corrections (typographical, formatting) do not increase the version number but are still logged. Prior versions are never silently rewritten; see Publication Methodology for the full detail on how corrections, retirement and supersession are handled.

Official guidance vs. Arena commentary vs. discussion

Three simple tests separate the three most common categories a reader will meet on Paradigm. If a claim can only be verified by checking a government or regulator publication, it belongs to official guidance — read it at the source, linked from every relevant page. If the claim is Arena's own reading of what that guidance means in practice, it belongs to Arena commentary — always labelled, always separated from the guidance text itself. If the claim is an argument, position or open question that goes beyond restating or interpreting a single piece of guidance, it belongs to a discussion paper or an emerging question — and is presented as exactly that: a contribution to debate, not a settled conclusion.
